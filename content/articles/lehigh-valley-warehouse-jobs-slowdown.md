Warehouse and distribution employment in the Lehigh Valley grew by less than 1 percent last year, according to regional labor market data, a sharp deceleration after a decade in which the sector added thousands of jobs annually and reshaped the area's economy.

Economists who track the region say the slowdown reflects both a maturing warehouse footprint, with fewer large parcels of developable land left near major highway interchanges, and broader efficiency gains from automation reducing headcount needs at newer facilities.

"We built an economy heavily dependent on square footage translating directly into jobs," said an economist at a regional planning organization. "That relationship is breaking down as newer buildings need fewer workers per square foot."

Local officials say they are trying to diversify the area's job base toward advanced manufacturing and life sciences, sectors that have grown modestly but from a much smaller base. Commercial real estate brokers report warehouse vacancy has ticked up for the first time in years, though it remains below the national average.

The Lehigh Valley Planning Commission plans to release a broader economic diversification study later this year.
