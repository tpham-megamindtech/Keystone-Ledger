A telehealth partnership between a network of rural primary care clinics and an urban academic medical center is letting patients in several northern-tier counties consult specialists by video from their local clinic, eliminating drives of two hours or more that many patients previously skipped rather than made.

The program equips participating clinics with video consultation rooms and dedicated broadband connections, addressing a common barrier where patients' home internet couldn't reliably support video calls even when the willingness to use telehealth existed.

"Distance was the single biggest reason patients missed specialist follow-ups," said a physician who helped design the program. "If we can bring the specialist to the local clinic instead of asking the patient to travel, show rates improve dramatically."

Early data from the program shows significantly higher specialist follow-up completion rates compared to referral patterns before the program launched, though administrators caution the comparison isn't a controlled study. The program currently covers cardiology, endocrinology, and psychiatric consultations, with plans to add dermatology given high demand for skin cancer screening in the largely outdoor-working rural population it serves.
