A South Philadelphia massage therapy clinic has spent the past year building referral relationships with physical therapists and primary care physicians, part of an effort to get more clients' sessions covered under health insurance rather than paid entirely out of pocket.

Clinic owner Grace Whitmore, a licensed massage therapist, says the effort requires convincing referring providers that massage therapy delivers measurable value for specific conditions like chronic lower back pain and post-surgical recovery, not simply relaxation.

"Insurance companies want documentation tied to a diagnosis, not a wellness goal," Whitmore said. "That's a different way of framing what we do, and it took real work with referring providers to build that pipeline."

The clinic now sees a mix of insurance-covered medically necessary sessions and traditional out-of-pocket wellness appointments, a hybrid model Whitmore says stabilizes revenue during slower wellness-booking periods like the middle of winter. She estimates roughly a quarter of current sessions are billed through insurance, up from none two years ago.

Whitmore cautions that the insurance billing process itself has added significant administrative overhead, requiring a part-time biller the clinic didn't previously need.
