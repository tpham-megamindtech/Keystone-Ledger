Adult education centers across several Pennsylvania counties report rising enrollment in GED preparation classes for a third consecutive year, a trend program coordinators attribute partly to immigrants seeking a recognized credential to qualify for better-paying jobs and partly to adults who left high school years earlier now facing employer requirements for a diploma equivalent.

"We're seeing a wider age range than we used to," said a coordinator at one regional adult education center. "It's not just recent dropouts anymore. We have people in their forties and fifties who need this credential for a job they've held for years but might lose without it."

Programs have added English-language support tracks alongside standard GED preparation to accommodate the growing number of English-language learners enrolling, requiring additional staffing that has strained some centers' budgets, which rely on a mix of state and federal adult education funding that has not kept pace with enrollment growth.

Completion rates vary significantly by center, with coordinators citing inconsistent transportation access and work-schedule conflicts as the most common reasons students who enroll ultimately don't complete the program.
