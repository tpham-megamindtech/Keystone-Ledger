Two neighboring Luzerne County school districts are moving toward a formal merger after years of informally sharing services like transportation and special education staffing, a step officials say has become necessary as enrollment continues to decline across the region.

Both districts have lost roughly 20 percent of their student population over the past fifteen years, driving up per-student administrative costs even as overall spending has stayed relatively flat. School boards from both districts have held joint public meetings to gauge community reaction, with responses described as mixed.

"Nobody loves the idea of losing their own school board or their own mascot," one district superintendent said. "But nobody loves the idea of cutting more electives and staff every year either."

A formal merger would require voter approval in both districts and a multi-year transition plan addressing everything from combined facility usage to labor contract harmonization between the districts' teacher unions. State education officials have offered technical assistance funding for districts pursuing voluntary consolidation, though final approval and a proposed timeline remain subject to both school boards' upcoming votes.
