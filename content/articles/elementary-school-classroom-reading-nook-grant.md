A statewide education nonprofit's micro-grant program has helped hundreds of Pennsylvania elementary school teachers rebuild classroom book collections this year, addressing a persistent gap that surveys show many teachers have historically covered using their own money.

Grants typically range from $200 to $500, enough to meaningfully refresh a classroom reading corner but modest by school budget standards, a scale the nonprofit says is intentional to keep the application process simple enough for busy teachers to complete quickly.

"We didn't want this to be another burdensome grant application that takes hours to write," the nonprofit's program director said. "A teacher can apply during a planning period."

Surveys conducted by the organization found that elementary teachers in high-poverty districts were both the most likely to have outdated or damaged classroom libraries and the most likely to have already spent significant personal money trying to address it. The program has distributed grants to teachers in 38 counties so far this year, with demand consistently exceeding available funding each application cycle. The nonprofit is seeking additional corporate sponsorship to expand the program's total budget for next year.
