Home health agencies serving homebound seniors across several Pennsylvania counties say they are turning away new clients or placing them on multi-week waitlists, citing an ongoing shortage of home health aides that has left approved care hours unstaffed even when families have insurance or Medicaid coverage already in place.

Agency directors say the shortage stems largely from wages that have not kept pace with retail and warehouse jobs offering comparable or better pay without the physically and emotionally demanding nature of home health work.

"We're competing for the same workers as a warehouse down the road that doesn't ask anyone to help someone bathe or manage medications," one agency director said. "Right now, the warehouse is winning that competition on pay alone."

Some agencies have raised wages where reimbursement rates allow, though Medicaid-funded home care rates are set by the state and have lagged behind rising labor costs in other sectors. Families unable to secure agency-provided care have increasingly turned to informal arrangements with independent caregivers, an option that avoids licensing requirements but also removes oversight and backup coverage that agencies typically provide when a caregiver is unavailable.
