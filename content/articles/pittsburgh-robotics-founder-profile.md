When Elena Marsh left a robotics research role to start her own automation company, investors kept asking the same question: why not move to Boston or the Bay Area? Her answer has become something of a talking point in Pittsburgh's startup circles.

"Every engineer I wanted to hire was already here," Marsh said. "I would have spent my first year of runway just trying to convince people to relocate."

Her company builds automated sorting arms for mid-sized warehouses, a market she says is underserved because most robotics investment chases larger distribution centers. The business has grown to 34 employees, most of them Carnegie Mellon or Pitt graduates who wanted to stay in the region.

Marsh is candid about the tradeoffs. Access to warehouse-automation customers required more travel than a Bay Area founder might face, and the fundraising process took longer without as many local venture firms writing large early checks.

"We had to be more disciplined about spending because the money came slower," she said. "I think that discipline is part of why we're still here."

The company closed a $9 million funding round late last year and plans to double its engineering team by the end of 2026.
