A rural Elk County school district has entered its second consecutive year without a permanent certified physics and chemistry teacher, relying instead on a rotation of long-term substitutes and a part-time retired teacher willing to commute from a neighboring county.

District officials say the vacancy reflects a broader pattern affecting rural Pennsylvania schools: certified science teachers, particularly in physics and upper-level chemistry, increasingly prefer positions in suburban districts offering higher pay and shorter commutes from larger population centers.

"We've posted this position through three hiring cycles," the district's superintendent said. "We get applicants who look qualified on paper and then decide against relocating once they see what's actually available here in terms of housing and amenities."

The district has offered a signing bonus and relocation stipend, unusual moves for a district of its size, without success so far. State education officials have flagged rural teacher shortages, particularly in STEM subjects, as a statewide concern, though proposed solutions like loan forgiveness programs for rural placements remain under legislative discussion rather than enacted policy.
