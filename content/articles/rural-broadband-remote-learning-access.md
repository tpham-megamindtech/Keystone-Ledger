Years after remote learning ended, several rural Pennsylvania school districts say inconsistent home internet access continues to limit students' ability to complete online homework assignments, a gap that state and federal broadband expansion programs have narrowed but not closed in the most sparsely populated counties.

Teachers in affected districts say they've adapted by designing assignments that don't strictly require internet access at home, printing materials or building in classroom completion time for anything requiring online research, workarounds that add planning burden but avoid penalizing students for infrastructure gaps outside their control.

"I can't assume every student has reliable internet at seven at night," said a high school teacher in a rural northern-tier district. "So I plan every assignment as if half my class doesn't."

State broadband expansion funding has connected new fiber lines to several previously unserved areas, but district technology coordinators say the buildout has proceeded unevenly, often reaching more densely populated pockets of a county before the most remote, lowest-density areas where the gap is often most acute. Districts have distributed mobile hotspots as a stopgap, though rural terrain and distance from cell towers limit their effectiveness in the hardest-to-reach areas.
