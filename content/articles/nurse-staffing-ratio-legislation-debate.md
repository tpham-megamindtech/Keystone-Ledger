Nursing unions have renewed a years-long legislative push for mandated minimum nurse-to-patient staffing ratios in Pennsylvania hospitals, reintroducing a bill that has stalled in previous sessions amid strong opposition from hospital industry associations.

Nurses testifying in support of the bill described routine assignments they say compromise patient safety, citing shifts where a single nurse managed patient loads well above what they consider safe for certain acuity levels.

"We're not asking for something radical," one intensive care nurse said in testimony. "We're asking for the same kind of ratio protections that have existed in other states for over twenty years."

Hospital associations argue mandated ratios, without accompanying investment in nursing school capacity, would simply force facilities to cut services or close beds during unavoidable short-staffing periods, disproportionately affecting smaller and rural hospitals least able to recruit additional staff on short notice. Both sides agree the underlying nursing shortage itself needs addressing but disagree sharply on whether mandated ratios help or worsen the problem in the near term. The bill remains in committee with no scheduled floor vote.
