Three Pennsylvania community colleges have begun requiring a personal finance course for graduation, after internal surveys found many students left with associate degrees but limited understanding of budgeting, credit scores, or how student loan interest accrues.

The course covers practical material: reading a pay stub, comparing loan terms, and building a basic monthly budget, rather than broader economic theory. Faculty who developed the curriculum say they deliberately avoided a textbook-heavy approach in favor of exercises using students' own finances.

"We had students who could explain supply and demand curves but didn't know their own credit utilization ratio," said one economics instructor involved in designing the course. "Those are very different skills."

Early feedback from students has been largely positive, though some faculty worry about adding a new graduation requirement to already tight two-year program schedules. The colleges plan to share outcome data, including loan default rates among graduates, after tracking the first cohort for two years.

Statewide community college associations are watching the pilot closely as a possible model for broader adoption.
