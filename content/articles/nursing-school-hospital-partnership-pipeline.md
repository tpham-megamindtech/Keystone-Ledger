A regional hospital system has partnered with a local nursing school to launch a tuition-free pipeline program, covering full nursing school costs for students who commit to working at the hospital for a set number of years after graduation and licensure.

The arrangement addresses two problems simultaneously, hospital administrators say: a persistent nursing shortage that has forced reliance on expensive traveling nurse contracts, and a nursing school that had space for more students than it could enroll given the cost barrier facing many prospective applicants.

"We were paying a premium for travel nurses that, over several years, would have covered nursing school tuition for a dozen students who'd have stayed permanently," the hospital's chief nursing officer said.

Students accepted into the program receive full tuition coverage and a stipend during clinical rotations, in exchange for a signed commitment to work at the hospital for a minimum of three years after graduation, with a prorated repayment obligation if they leave earlier. The first cohort of fifteen students began this academic year, with the hospital planning to expand the program if early retention outcomes meet projections.
