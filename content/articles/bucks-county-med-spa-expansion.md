Radiance Med Spa in Bucks County has hired a part-time supervising physician and updated its treatment protocols after Pennsylvania regulators increased inspection activity at medical aesthetic practices statewide, part of a broader response to safety complaints reported at spas in other states.

Owner Kathleen Ortiz, a nurse practitioner who founded the spa seven years ago, says the business already operated within existing supervision requirements but decided additional physician involvement was worth the cost given increased scrutiny.

"I'd rather be over-compliant than have a single incident that puts clients at risk or threatens the business," Ortiz said.

The spa offers injectables, laser treatments, and body contouring services, a category of treatments regulators have flagged for inconsistent oversight nationally as demand has grown faster than clear regulatory frameworks in some states. Ortiz says she supports clearer state-level licensing standards specific to medical spas, arguing the current patchwork of general medical practice rules doesn't address aesthetic treatments well.

The spa has not had any adverse incident reports since opening, according to state licensing records Ortiz shared with a reporter.
