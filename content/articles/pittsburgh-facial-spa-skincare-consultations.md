Several Pittsburgh-area facial spas have added standalone, extended skincare consultation appointments to their service menus, responding to clients who arrive overwhelmed by conflicting skincare advice circulating on social media and unsure which treatments actually suit their skin.

"People come in with a list of five products they saw recommended online, half of which shouldn't be used together," said an esthetician at a Squirrel Hill spa offering the new consultation service. "We spend forty-five minutes just building an actual skin assessment before recommending anything."

The consultations are priced as a standalone service rather than bundled into a facial, a shift spa owners say required convincing clients that paying for advice alone, without a treatment included, was worth the cost. Early bookings suggest a meaningful share of consultation clients return afterward for treatments recommended during the session, offsetting the standalone service's lower margin.

Spa owners say the trend reflects a broader shift toward skepticism of generic online skincare advice, with clients increasingly willing to pay for individualized professional guidance rather than trial-and-error based on trending products.
