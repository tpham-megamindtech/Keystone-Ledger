Several spas across the Lehigh Valley say they have capped new client bookings not because demand has slowed, but because they cannot find enough licensed massage therapists to fill open appointment shifts, a staffing gap owners describe as the tightest they've seen in over a decade.

"I have the schedule capacity for twelve more appointments a week," said the owner of a Bethlehem-area spa. "I just don't have the therapists to fill them, and I've had the job posting up for months."

Massage therapy schools in the region report steady but not growing enrollment, while therapist turnover has increased as more graduates move into physical therapy clinics or chiropractic offices offering more predictable hours and benefits than typical spa employment.

Some spa owners have responded by raising therapist pay and offering benefits previously uncommon in the industry, including paid time off and retirement contributions, hoping to compete more directly with healthcare-adjacent employers. Industry associations say the shortage mirrors broader healthcare worker shortages affecting the region, since massage therapy licensing shares some regulatory overlap with other bodywork professions.
