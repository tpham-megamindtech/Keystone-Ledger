A growing number of hairstylists in York are leaving commission-based salon jobs to rent individual suites at a shared studio building, part of a national shift in how beauty services are delivered that has reached the region more slowly than larger metro areas.

The suite model lets stylists operate as independent business owners, setting their own prices and hours in exchange for a flat monthly rent rather than splitting revenue with a salon owner. For experienced stylists with an established client base, the math can work out favorably.

"I went from giving up half of every service to keeping all of it, minus a fixed rent," said one stylist who moved into a suite last year after nine years at a traditional salon. "The tradeoff is I also handle my own booking, supplies, and taxes now."

Not every stylist has found the transition smooth. Suite operators say newer stylists without an existing client base have struggled more, since the model offers none of the walk-in traffic a storefront salon can generate. The suite building's owner says occupancy has grown from six to nineteen stylists over three years, with a waitlist for remaining units.
