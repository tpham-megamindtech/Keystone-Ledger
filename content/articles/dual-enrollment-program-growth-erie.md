Dual enrollment participation among Erie-area high school students has roughly doubled over the past four years, according to figures from a regional college that partners with several area districts to offer college-level courses taught by credentialed high school instructors.

The growth follows expanded state funding aimed at reducing dual enrollment costs for families, who previously had to cover tuition for college credit courses even when taken during the regular school day at their own high school.

"Cost was the biggest barrier we heard from families," said a coordinator overseeing the partnership program. "Once state funding covered a meaningful share of that cost, participation took off almost immediately."

Participating students can graduate high school with a semester or more of transferable college credit, potentially reducing time and cost to a bachelor's degree. School counselors report the program has been particularly popular among first-generation college-bound students, whose families are often especially cost-sensitive to the overall price of a four-year degree. Several districts are now working to expand course offerings beyond the current slate of introductory-level classes.
