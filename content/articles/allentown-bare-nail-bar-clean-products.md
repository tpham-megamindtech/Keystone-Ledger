Bare Nail Bar in Allentown has built its entire brand identity around a feature most nail salons treat as an afterthought: air quality. The shop installed a commercial-grade ventilation system uncommon in salons its size and uses only nail polish formulas free of the most commonly flagged industry chemicals.

"Nail technicians breathe these fumes eight hours a day, five days a week," said owner Priya Chandra, herself a former technician who developed respiratory issues at a previous job. "I built this salon for my staff's health first, and it turned out clients cared about that too."

The ventilation investment added meaningfully to build-out costs, and clean product formulations often run more expensive than conventional alternatives, a cost Chandra has passed through modestly in pricing. She says the positioning has attracted a specific customer base willing to pay slightly more, including several clients who avoided salons altogether due to chemical sensitivities.

Chandra has begun consulting informally with other salon owners interested in similar ventilation retrofits, though she notes the upfront cost remains a barrier for smaller shops operating on thin margins.
